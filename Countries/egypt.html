City Dust and Ocean Currents

Navigating the beautiful, relentless chaos of Cairo, seeking underwater serenity while diving the coral reefs of Dahab, and slowly crossing the Gulf of Aqaba via the Nuweibaa ferry.

The Sensory Overload: Cairo

Egypt hits you all at once. Cairo is an absolute marvel of sensory overload—a sprawling, ancient metropolis where the heavy scent of exhaust fumes mixes with roasting spices, and the relentless blaring of car horns competes with the hauntingly beautiful call to prayer echoing from thousands of minarets. It is a city that never pauses. While the history is staggering, merely crossing the street here feels like a high-stakes adventure.

The Deep Blue: Diving in Dahab

Trading the smog and traffic of the capital for the laid-back, bohemian shores of the Sinai Peninsula was a massive relief. Dahab operates on an entirely different frequency. Here, the focus shifts entirely to the water. The Red Sea offers some of the most spectacular, high-visibility diving on the planet. Strapping on a tank and sinking below the surface to explore the vibrant, teeming coral reefs and the sheer, dizzying drop-offs of the surrounding dive sites provided a profound, weightless silence that stood in perfect contrast to the noise of Cairo.

The Crossing: Nuweibaa Ferry

To continue the journey out of Egypt, I headed further up the coast to the quiet, dusty port town of Nuweibaa. Taking the ferry across the Red Sea (technically traversing the Gulf of Aqaba) is an exercise in pure patience. It’s a beautifully slow, methodical way to travel—waiting in un-airconditioned terminal halls, going through stacks of paperwork, and eventually standing on the open deck, feeling the deep sea breeze as the rugged, sun-scorched mountains of the Sinai slowly fade into the horizon behind you.

Koshary and Bedouin Tea

You cannot survive the pace of Egypt without heavy fuel. In Cairo, the undisputed king of street food is Koshary—a massive, intensely filling bowl of lentils, macaroni, and rice, smothered in a spicy, garlicky tomato sauce and topped with crispy fried onions. It is pure, unadulterated carbohydrate energy. Down on the coast in Dahab and Nuweibaa, the diet shifts gracefully to freshly caught, perfectly grilled Red Sea fish, always followed by small, scalding-hot glasses of heavily sweetened Bedouin mint tea to cut through the ocean breeze.
