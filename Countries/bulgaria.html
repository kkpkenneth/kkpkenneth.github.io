Thracian Gold and the Black Sea Coast

Peeling back the layers of history in the capital of Sofia, walking the ancient Roman amphitheater of Plovdiv, and tracing the Black Sea to the vibrant coastal hub of Burgas.

Advertisement

Bulgaria is a massive, multi-layered crossroads of civilizations in the Balkans. It is a place where you can find ancient Roman ruins sitting casually underneath brutalist Soviet-era metro stations. Documenting this contrast required swapping gear constantly: using the wide-angle Canon to capture the golden domes of the massive Orthodox cathedrals, and relying on the Xiaomi to snap quick, candid shots while navigating the lively pedestrian streets and relaxed coastal promenades.

The Urban Layers: Sofia and Plovdiv

The journey naturally began in Sofia, a capital city resting at the foot of Vitosha Mountain. It’s a city of immense historical depth—literally. When excavating for the modern metro system, they uncovered an entire ancient Roman city (Serdica), which is now seamlessly integrated into the underground stations. Standing before the colossal, neo-Byzantine Alexander Nevsky Cathedral is a humbling architectural experience.

Heading southeast brings you to Plovdiv, one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Europe. Built on seven hills, it boasts a perfectly preserved 1st-century Roman amphitheater that is still used for performances today. The city’s energy is anchored in the "Kapana" (The Trap) district—a dense, pedestrian-only maze of narrow, winding streets packed with colorful street art, craft beer bars, and artisan cafes.

The Black Sea Coast and Burgas

Leaving the inland mountains behind, the expedition shifted east to the sweeping coastline of the Black Sea. The anchor for this coastal leg was Burgas, a major port city that feels wonderfully relaxed compared to the massive, crowded resort towns further north. Strolling through the vast Sea Garden (Morska gradina) and walking out onto the iconic pier as the sun sets over the water is the perfect way to wind down. It serves as an excellent, vibrant gateway to the southern coast, smoothly mixing modern urban energy with laid-back beach life.

Banitsa, Shopska Salad, and Tarator

Bulgarian cuisine is fresh, deeply comforting, and heavily reliant on incredibly high-quality dairy and produce. Mornings strictly demand a slice of warm Banitsa—a flaky, buttery pastry layered with whisked eggs and crumbled sirene (a tangy, white brine cheese similar to feta). For lunch, the quintessential national dish is the bright and refreshing Shopska Salad, composed of diced tomatoes, cucumbers, onions, and peppers, completely buried under a mountain of grated sirene cheese. And on hot summer days along the Black Sea coast, a bowl of Tarator (a cold soup made from yogurt, cucumbers, garlic, and dill) is the absolute perfect way to cool down after a long hike.
