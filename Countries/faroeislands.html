Faroe Islands

Travel Guide & Archive

The Mystical Isles of Waterfalls and Sheep

A dramatic North Atlantic road trip through rugged peaks and floating lakes.

Advertisement

The journey to the Faroe Islands began on the turbulent waters of the North Atlantic. We took a ferry from Hirtshals, charting a course that swept right past the rugged Shetland islands. The ocean's powerful swells made their presence known; the waves inside the ferry’s swimming pool were incredibly vigorous, turning a simple swim into a thrilling, chaotic ride dictated by the movement of the ship. We finally arrived in the capital, Tórshavn, under the veil of night, allowing the anticipation of this mystical archipelago to settle in.

The very next day, we wasted no time and kicked things off with a bracing hike from Gundadalur, winding our way through the spectacular, sweeping Faroese terrain until ending our trek near Hvitisandur. It was the perfect introduction to the wild, untamed nature of these islands.

Waterfalls, Sheep, and the Floating Lake

The quintessential Faroe Islands road trip began the following morning. We drove through a landscape that felt almost aggressively beautiful, passing the charming settlements of Kollafjørður, Haldarsvik, Runavik, and Kaldbak. It felt as though we were constantly crossing an endless tapestry of cascading waterfalls and dodging curious flocks of sheep that roamed freely across the asphalt. A modern marvel awaited us on our route as we drove through the incredible underwater roundabout—an illuminated, sub-oceanic junction that felt entirely futuristic.

We then set out on a hike from Miðvágur to Sandavágur. The trail culminated in one of the most iconic optical illusions in the natural world: arriving at Sørvágsvatn, the famous "floating lake" of the Faroe Islands. Perched dramatically above the churning ocean cliffs, the lake appears to defy gravity, offering a view that anchors itself permanently into your memory.

Fishing Towns, Heights, and History

Our third day took us driving past Klaksvík, where we savored their famously fresh fish and chips before tackling the steep but rewarding hike up Klakkur. The panoramic views of the neighboring fjords from the summit were staggering. As night fell, we drove to Vestmanna to catch an energetic local handball match, a brilliant way to immerse ourselves in the tight-knit island community spirit away from the typical tourist trails.

The subsequent day was a relentless visual feast. We drove from Syðrugøta to Eiði, then onto Funningur to embark on a spectacular hike at Hvithamar. After descending from the dizzying heights, we journeyed to Gjógv to admire its charming timber church, quietly standing since 1929 against the coastal winds. Heading back to Tórshavn, we explored the historic old Faroese parliament buildings in Tinganes, their red-painted timber glowing in the evening light. We ended the night tasting traditional Turrur Fiskur (dried fish) washed down with a local Palleba beer at the lively Irish Pub in town.

A Mystical Departure

On our last day on the Faroe Islands, we headed south towards Sandur in the crisp morning air before looping back to Tórshavn for a final, comforting meal of Torske chips paired with a refreshing Slupp beer. Though it wasn't the right season to see the adorable puffins in the wild, we were still able to view fascinating specimens at Tjóðsavnið, the Faroe Islands National Museum in Hoyvík.

We took a final stroll out to the lighthouse in Tórshavn. Looking out over the water, we could see from afar that our ferry was incoming. It was time to go. The Faroe Islands proved to be such a dramatic, mystical archipelago that it leaves a permanent imprint on the soul. I would love to return—next time, without a doubt, during puffin season.
