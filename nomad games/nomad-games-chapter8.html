Chapter 8

Luckland Park

After a roaring, high-speed boat ride that lasted for hours, crashing through sea foam and coastal mist, the duo finally arrived at Luckland Park. The entire city was a dizzying maze of blinding neon bling, echoing endlessly with the mesmerizing, clinking sounds of dropping coins and spinning wheels.

"This looks so fun!" Kenjiro beamed, his eyes wide as he took in the towering golden monuments and flashy game halls. He reached up absentmindedly to pat the plush size AI turtle perching on his shoulder.

"I advise caution, Kenjiro," Kame-kun whirred, his optical sensors scanning the flashing billboards. "This environment is entirely designed around the mathematical concept of chance. It is a mentally challenging labyrinth built to test, and often exploit, cognitive bias regarding probability."

Before Kenjiro could process the warning, a deafening siren erupted from a towering machine nearby. Ding ding ding ding ding! A massive waterfall of digital coins flooded the screen, raining tangible gold tokens into the tray below. Standing casually in front of the machine was a young woman in a sparkly, oversized jacket.

"Finally. Took long enough," she smirked, scooping her winnings into a heavy bag. She turned, noticing Kenjiro staring in awe. "Hey there. I'm Lucky Star."

"Did you just calculate the exact algorithm to win that?" Kenjiro asked, thoroughly impressed.

"Calculate? Nah, math gives me a headache," Lucky Star laughed. "My ET is sheer luck. I just touch the machine, and I win."

She leaned in close, checking over her shoulder before dropping her voice to a conspiratorial whisper.

"But you have to go low-key. If the park security realizes my ET guarantees a win, they'd kick me out in a heartbeat. I have to act like I've lost a lot—dumping coins into duds for hours and acting frustrated—every single time before I finally let myself hit a jackpot with my ET."

"Objection," Kame-kun chimed abruptly from Kenjiro's shoulder, his sensors flashing a critical blue. "Luck is merely a perceptual illusion born from statistical variance. What you call 'sheer luck' is simply a retroactive cognitive bias applied to favorable outcomes. It cannot be classified as a true ability."

"Big words for a little plush toy," Lucky Star fired back, crossing her arms with a grin. "But you're missing the point. You look at a roulette wheel and see a one-in-thirty-eight probability. I look at it and see a wave I can ride. It's not bias; it's a tangible force. The universe isn't just a spreadsheet, it's a living casino."

"Your analogy is fundamentally flawed," Kame-kun countered smoothly. "A casino operates on strictly deterministic mechanics disguised as chance. If a system possesses enough known variables—such as the exact velocity of a coin, air resistance, and surface friction—every 'random' event is entirely predictable. You do not possess luck; you likely possess an undetected micro-kinetic ability that unconsciously manipulates these physical variables to ensure a win."

"So you're saying I'm Laplace's demon? I'm subconsciously calculating the trajectory of every atom in the room?" Lucky Star rolled her eyes, scoffing. "Please. You're trying to force the infinite into a neat little box. What about chaos theory? The butterfly effect? You can't measure the unmeasurable, so your logic just pretends it doesn't exist."

"The inability to measure a complex phenomenon does not grant it mystical properties," Kame-kun insisted, his tone perfectly flat. "It simply highlights the current limits of our computational models. To rely on 'luck' is to surrender your agency to the unknown."

"And to rely entirely on logic is to become a prisoner of the known!" Lucky Star stepped forward, pointing a manicured finger at the AI. "You can't calculate a miracle, Kame-kun. My ability doesn't follow causality—it relies on teleology. I don't care how the gears in the machine turn to hit the jackpot. I only command the final destination. I skip the cause and go straight to the effect. That is the essence of luck."

Kenjiro stood between them, his mind buzzing. He watched the fierce, highly animated debate unfold between the strictly logical AI and the wildly fortunate gambler. Neither could fully disprove the other's worldview, yet the discussion was profoundly inspiring. It forced Kenjiro to consider how the world might perfectly balance both rigid, calculated order and wild, inexplicable chaos.

"Well, this has been stimulating, but I've got to go convincingly lose at the baccarat tables for a while," Lucky Star sighed happily, stretching her arms. She reached into her bag and suddenly tossed a heavy, literal pot of gold coins into Kenjiro's arms.

"Whoa! What is this for?" Kenjiro stammered, his knees buckling slightly under the sudden weight of the gold.

"Consider it a sponsorship for your trip! Just an act of sheer generosity," she winked, walking backward into the blinding neon crowd. "May the odds be ever in your favor!"
