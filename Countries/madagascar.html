The Red Island

An exploration of the cosmopolitan heights of Antananarivo, the surreal majesty of the Avenue of the Baobabs, the rare nocturnal wildlife of Aye-aye Island, the lush rainforests of Andasibe, and the coastal gateways of Toamasina and Morondava—a land defined by its profound biodiversity, ancient traditions, and a deeply singular evolutionary history.

The Capital: Antananarivo

Perched high on a ridge, Antananarivo is a captivating cosmopolitan hub that perfectly showcases a blend of rich architectural influences and historic character. My time here was defined by the stunning purple blooms of the Jacaranda mimosifolia trees that decorate the city, as well as a true culinary adventure. I began every morning with my favorite local breakfast, Vary amin'anana, a savory rice porridge cooked with fresh leafy greens, garlic, ginger, and onions. I explored the city's deeper cultural tastes through Romazava, the national dish featuring a fragrant stew of various meats and local greens; Marmite Vezo, a rich fish-based stew highlighting the catch of the day with aromatic spices; and Koba, a dense and sticky sweet treat made from ground peanuts, brown sugar, and rice flour wrapped in banana leaves. No visit here would be complete without savoring the local Vanilla liqueur, a perfect reflection of the island's world-famous botanical wealth, infused with premium bourbon vanilla beans.

Iconic Landscapes: Avenue of the Baobabs

Located in the Menabe region, the Avenue of the Baobabs features majestic Grandidier's baobabs—the "mothers of the forest"—lining the road between Morondava and Belon'i Tsiribihina. Nearby, the Baobab Amoureux showcases two trees twisted together over centuries, a must-see landmark for travelers exploring western Madagascar.

Biodiversity: Andasibe & Aye-aye Island

The lush rainforests of Andasibe-Mantadia National Park are home to the vocal indri lemurs. Further east, remote Aye-aye Island offers rare opportunities to observe the mysterious nocturnal aye-aye lemurs. Nearby, the small village of Andranokoditra serves as a tranquil base for exploring the unique flora and fauna of the Pangalanes Canal system.

Coastal Gateways: Toamasina & Morondava

Toamasina (Tamatave) on the east coast is a major maritime hub characterized by old town Creole houses built on stilts and a scenic seaside boulevard. On the west coast, Morondava acts as a traditional port city and the essential starting point for excursions into the iconic western landscapes and the towering forests of the interior.
