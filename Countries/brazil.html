Brazil

Jungles, Concrete, and Coastlines

A massive expedition traversing the urban density of São Paulo, the Amazonian depths of Manaus, the wetlands of Miranda and the Pantanal, the roar of Foz do Iguaçu, the beaches of Florianópolis, and the unique split border of Uruguay and Brazil.

Brazil is not just a country; it operates on the scale of a continent. The geographical diversity is staggering, meaning the camera gear was constantly adapting. I relied heavily on the Sony for low-light captures deep in the Amazonian canopy, the Canon with a fast lens for the bustling energy of the coastal cities, and the Xiaomi for quickly grabbing street scenes in the dense urban jungles without missing a beat.

The Megacities: Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo

The journey anchored in the two titans of the southeast. Rio de Janeiro is arguably the most beautifully situated city on earth, where sheer granite peaks crash directly into the Atlantic Ocean. Capturing the sweeping views from Sugarloaf Mountain or beneath the iconic Christ the Redeemer is breathtaking. In stark contrast, São Paulo is an endless, intimidating ocean of concrete and skyscrapers. It is a cultural powerhouse, deeply defined by its incredible street art, world-class gastronomy, and a relentless, high-speed metropolitan energy that never sleeps.

The Wild Heart: Manaus, Miranda, and the Pantanal

Flying north into the heat and humidity, Manaus serves as the gritty, fascinating gateway into the Amazon rainforest, perfectly situated where the dark waters of the Rio Negro meet the sandy Amazon River. Yet, for pure wildlife viewing, the expedition headed to the Pantanal, accessing the world’s largest tropical wetland via the outpost of Miranda. This region is a photographer's absolute dream. Unlike the dense jungle canopy of the Amazon, the open marshes here provide unparalleled opportunities to track capybaras, caimans, toucans, and the elusive, magnificent jaguar.

The South: Foz do Iguaçu, Curitiba, and Florianópolis

Moving south, the sheer, violent power of nature is on full display at Foz do Iguaçu. Standing on the boardwalks as millions of gallons of water thunder over the horseshoe cliffs of the Devil's Throat leaves you entirely drenched and humbled. Continuing through the southern states, the green, highly organized urban planning of Curitiba offered a breath of fresh air before hitting the stunning, surf-swept island beaches of Florianópolis, a haven for coastal relaxation.

The Border Town: Crossing into Uruguay

The Brazilian leg of the journey wrapped up with a fascinating overland crossing at the far southern tip. Arriving at the border town of Chuí/Chuy, you find a unique geographical oddity: a single avenue physically splits the town down the middle, where one sidewalk is in Brazil and the other is in Uruguay. It is a seamless, fascinating cultural blend and the perfect exit point into the rest of the continent.

Feijoada, Pão de Queijo, and the Caipirinha

Brazilian food is robust, deeply flavorful, and incredibly diverse. The absolute anchor of the national diet is Feijoada—a rich, dark, slow-cooked stew of black beans and various cuts of pork, typically served with farofa (toasted cassava flour) and orange slices to cut the richness. For a quick snack on the go, whether waiting for a bus in São Paulo or a boat in Manaus, nothing beats a warm, chewy Pão de Queijo (cheese bread). And naturally, after a long day of exploring the wetlands or the coast, sitting back with an ice-cold, perfectly muddled lime and cachaça Caipirinha is the ultimate, non-negotiable Brazilian ritual.
