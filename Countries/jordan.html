Rose City Sands

An expedition through the shifting landscapes of the Middle East, from the deep blue reefs of Aqaba, into the sweeping Martian deserts of Wadi Rum, and finally passing through the bustling hub of Wadi Musa to discover the ancient, rock-carved majesty of Petra.

The Red Sea Gateway: Aqaba

Starting at the southern tip, Aqaba offers a brilliant splash of blue against the arid desert backdrop. As Jordan's only coastal city, the Red Sea here is a world-class destination for scuba diving and snorkeling. Plunging into the warm waters reveals incredibly diverse marine biology and vibrant coral gardens. Swimming along the drop-offs, it is the perfect environment to scan the deep blue for graceful rays gliding by or the occasional blacktip reef shark patrolling the depths.

Valley of the Moon: Wadi Rum

Leaving the coast and heading northeast brings you into the sweeping, red expanse of Wadi Rum. This towering, monolithic desert landscape feels entirely otherworldly. Traversing the dunes in the back of a 4x4, surrounded by sheer granite and sandstone mountains, gives you a true sense of scale. Spending the night in a traditional Bedouin camp, completely disconnected from the grid and sleeping under a blindingly clear canopy of stars, is an absolute highlight of the region.

The Nabataean Capital: Wadi Musa & Petra

Wadi Musa serves as the energetic, bustling gateway town that leads directly into one of the world's greatest archaeological wonders: Petra. Walking down the winding, narrow gorge of the Siq—with its soaring sandstone walls changing colors in the shifting light—builds an incredible sense of anticipation. Emerging from the canyon to suddenly face the monumental, intricately carved facade of Al-Khazneh (The Treasury) is a moment that never loses its impact, no matter how many photographs you have seen.

The Ultimate Bedouin Feast: Mansaf

You cannot truly experience Jordanian culture without sharing a massive platter of Mansaf. Deeply rooted in Bedouin tradition, this national dish consists of tender lamb cooked in a rich, tangy broth made from fermented dried yogurt called jameed. Served over a mountain of rice and thin flatbread, and generously garnished with toasted almonds and pine nuts, it is a communal feast that perfectly captures the profound hospitality of the country.
