Chapter 2

THE NIGHT MARKET

The air in the Green Island night market was thick with the mouth-watering aromas of "small sausage in large sausage" (sticky rice sausage wrapped around a grilled pork sausage) and freshly baked "pepper buns" (crispy dough filled with peppery pork and scallions), as Kame-kun and Kenjiro navigated the bustling stalls. Kame-kun paused at a vendor serving these delicacies, gesturing with a skewer toward the vibrant, shifting crowd.

"You know, Kenjiro," Kame-kun said, sampling a piece of the savory sausage, "every traveler in the games is a 'hunter' of sorts, tracking different prey across the globe. Each traveler follows a specific mission based on their type."

"So, you're saying our 'hunt' here makes us a specific type?"

"Exactly. By being here to experience the regional culinary traditions, we are currently acting as Gastro-travelers. But look at the others. It’s a vast, interconnected hunt."

"Think of it this way: Gastro-travelers hunt for culinary authenticity and regional specialties to understand a destination's identity. Eco-travelers hunt for fragile ecosystems to study and protect, while Geo-travelers hunt for remote territories to complete their global map and document their presence in diverse landscapes. Photo-travelers, the 'travelers of light,' hunt for the perfect intersection of time and illumination to capture the visual aesthetics of architecture and scenery. Athlo-travelers hunt for the intensity of competition, whether attending high-stakes matches or participating in local sports. Historio-travelers hunt for the remnants of a bygone era by standing exactly where historical events occurred. Heorto-travelers hunt for the peak of cultural energy, syncing their journeys to immerse themselves in major celebrations and public rituals. Periegeto-travelers hunt for the thrill of remote expeditions or specialized exploration, often utilizing specific skills to navigate their environment. Polito-travelers hunt for an understanding of the systems, governance, and civic identities that shape a destination. Finally, Dromo-travelers hunt for the heartbeat of a region’s transit systems, studying how connectivity and infrastructure shape a culture."

"I see. They're all hunting for something that isn't always physical, aren't they?"

"Precisely. We choose our destinations based on what our specific mission demands us to find."
