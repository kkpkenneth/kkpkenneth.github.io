Himalayan Heights and Holy Sites

An extensive exploration of the buzzing, temple-filled capital of Kathmandu, the serene lakeside beauty of Pokhara leading to the breathtaking sunrise views from Poonhill, the deeply sacred Buddhist pilgrimage site of Lumbini, and the unforgettable aerial spectacle of spotting Mount Everest from an inbound flight from Bhutan.

The Cultural Basin: Kathmandu

The Kathmandu Valley is a chaotic, vibrant sensory overload where ancient spirituality permeates daily life. The capital city of Kathmandu is a mesmerizing maze of narrow alleyways, hidden courtyards, and centuries-old architecture. From the intricately carved wooden temples of Durbar Square to the deeply profound, incense-shrouded atmospheres of the Swayambhunath (Monkey Temple) and Boudhanath stupas, the city is a living museum. It serves as the ultimate introduction to a nation that sits seamlessly at the crossroads of Hinduism and Buddhism.

Gateway to the Annapurnas: Pokhara & Poonhill

Leaving the hectic capital behind, Pokhara offers a breath of fresh, alpine air. Situated along the tranquil shores of Phewa Lake with the jagged peaks of the Machapuchare reflecting in the water, it is the premier gateway for Himalayan trekking. From here, the multi-day trek to Poonhill begins. Hiking through dense rhododendron forests and traditional Gurung villages culminates in one of the most rewarding viewpoints in the world. Standing atop Poonhill at dawn to watch the first rays of the sun ignite the snow-capped summits of the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges is an intensely humbling and majestic experience.

The Birthplace of the Buddha: Lumbini

Traveling south to the flat, dusty plains of the Terai region brings you to Lumbini, one of the world's most important spiritual pilgrimage destinations. Recognized as the birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama (Lord Buddha), the area is a vast, peaceful monastic zone. Walking through the sacred gardens, sitting quietly by the Maya Devi Temple, and seeing the ancient Ashoka Pillar offers a deeply introspective contrast to the towering mountains found in the north of the country. Dozens of monasteries built by nations from around the globe dot the landscape, creating a universal atmosphere of peace.

The Roof of the World: Sighting Mount Everest

Sometimes, the greatest views in Nepal do not require days of strenuous trekking. The arrival into the country can be a spectacle in its own right. While taking the regional flight from Bhutan into Kathmandu, gazing out the right side of the airplane window reveals the ultimate prize: a crystal-clear, aerial view of Mount Everest. Seeing the dark, jagged pyramid of the world's highest peak piercing through the ocean of white clouds from cruising altitude is a thrilling, once-in-a-lifetime introduction to the sheer, unparalleled scale of the Himalayas.
