Georgia

The Balcony of the Caucasus: A Georgian Expedition

A journey through the ancient stone pillars, vibrant hostel life, and culinary depths of Tbilisi and beyond.

Advertisement

Nestled in the deep valleys of the Caucasus Mountains, Georgia is a country where the borders of Europe and Asia blur into something entirely unique. Across multiple expeditions—most notably in May 2022 and the spring of 2026—this nation revealed itself as a place of profound hospitality, rugged landscapes, and deep Orthodox history. It is a country that feels simultaneously ancient and vibrantly youthful, defined as much by its soaring mountain monasteries as its late-night bar culture in the capital.

Monolithic Faith and the Old City

The historical weight of Georgia is perhaps best physically manifested outside the city limits. A journey into the mountainous interior brings travelers to the Katskhi pillar, an awe-inspiring natural limestone monolith that juts 40 meters into the sky. Atop this sheer rock face sits a tiny, ancient hermitage, a testament to the isolation and dedication of early Christian monks. Standing at the base of this pillar provides a dizzying sense of the region's spiritual history.

In stark contrast to the quiet isolation of the mountains is the bustling heart of Tbilisi. The capital city is a beautiful, chaotic mix of traditional wooden balconies, winding cobblestone streets, and modern hubs like the Galleria. The architecture here tells the story of a city that has been rebuilt dozens of times over centuries, yet has never lost its deeply entrenched cultural identity or its legendary tradition of welcoming strangers.

The Gentle Giants and Street Companions

The pace of life in Georgia is often dictated by its animals. High up on the winding mountain passes, traffic regularly comes to a complete standstill to accommodate the slow, unbothered herds of cows that freely roam and graze along the steep roadsides. They belong to the landscape as much as the mountains themselves.

Down in Tbilisi, the urban wildlife is equally memorable. The city is famous for its large population of street dogs. Unlike in many other parts of the world, these ear-tagged canines are incredibly gentle, community-cared-for animals. They are a constant, peaceful presence, often found lounging lazily in the warm sun on busy sidewalks or finding a cool, quiet corner to sleep inside the airport terminals, embodying the relaxed pace of the city.

The Georgian Table: Dumplings, Bread, and Wine

To travel through Georgia without surrendering to its gastronomy is to miss the soul of the country. The Georgian table, or *supra*, is an event of abundance. The meals are heavy, comforting, and designed to be shared. The undisputed king of the menu is **khachapuri**, the legendary boat-shaped bread overflowing with molten cheese and topped with a runny egg. Not to be outdone is **kubdari**, a dense, savory pastry packed with spiced meat that provides perfect fuel for long mountain hikes.

Equally essential are **khinkali**—large, beautifully pleated dumplings filled with spiced meat and a rich, hot broth. Eating them requires a specific technique: grabbing the thick doughy knob, biting a small hole to drink the broth, and devouring the rest. These carb-heavy feasts are invariably accompanied by **saperavi wine**, a deep, dark red wine born from one of the oldest viticulture traditions in the world. Exploring the local wine museums reveals that Georgians have been fermenting grapes in underground clay vessels, known as *qvevri*, for over 8,000 years.

Hostels, Train Rides, and Midnight Creativity

The social dynamic of Georgia shifts as the sun sets. Basing out of youth hostels in Tbilisi provides a perfect, energetic launching pad for late-night exploration. The pub and bar scene in the capital is thriving, offering a warm, dimly lit refuge where travelers and locals mix freely over drinks and conversation.

But the social and creative energy isn't confined to the bars. Some of the most distinct memories are forged in transit. Long, rhythmic train rides across the rugged countryside become impromptu art studios—sketching and drawing in notebooks as the mountainous silhouette of the Caucasus slowly rolls past the window. It is in these quiet, creative moments between destinations that the true, enduring character of Georgia takes root in the traveler's mind.
