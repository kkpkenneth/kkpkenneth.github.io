Broken Hearts and Adriatic Luxury

Wandering through the bittersweet exhibits in the capital of Zagreb before heading south to celebrate a milestone birthday in absolute luxury along the ancient, walled coastline of Dubrovnik.

Advertisement

Croatia offers a brilliant dual personality, splitting its charm between historic, café-lined inland cities and some of the most striking, crystalline coastlines in Europe. Balancing this meant swapping the Canon wide-angle lens for sweeping shots of the dramatic Adriatic cliffs, keeping the Xiaomi handy for weaving through narrow medieval alleyways, and relying entirely on the Sony's low-light capabilities to capture the intimate, dimly lit details of the capital's indoor exhibits.

The Intimate Capital: Zagreb

The journey began away from the beaches in the vibrant, highly walkable capital of Zagreb. It possesses a distinct, relaxed Central European energy, defined by its funiculars, grand architecture, and endless outdoor café culture. The absolute standout experience here, however, was stepping into the beautifully melancholic Museum of Broken Relationships. This deeply human, universally relatable exhibition is filled with donated personal objects—ranging from the mundane to the bizarre—left over from failed relationships, accompanied by the raw, often heartbreaking stories of the people who submitted them.

The Pearl of the Adriatic: Dubrovnik

Heading south along the dramatic Dalmatian coast brings you to Dubrovnik. Walking the massive, ancient stone walls that ring the Old Town as the sun dips below the horizon is a cinematic, non-negotiable experience. But this specific leg of the expedition was also about hitting pause to celebrate a birthday in proper style.

To mark the occasion, I checked into the Rixos Premium Dubrovnik. After months of intense, rugged travel, stepping into pure luxury was the ultimate reset. The day was spent unwinding in their world-class spa, quietly relaxing in the elegant tea lounge, and finally toasting to another year with a glass of champagne on a balcony, staring out at a sweeping, uninterrupted view of the turquoise Adriatic Sea.

Štrukli, Crni Rižot, and Peka

Croatian cuisine effortlessly bridges hearty Central European fare and fresh Mediterranean diets. In Zagreb, grabbing a warm, comforting portion of baked Štrukli (a savory dough filled with cottage cheese and sour cream) is mandatory. Down on the coast, the menu shifts entirely to the sea. You cannot leave the Dalmatian region without trying Crni Rižot, an intensely rich, savory black risotto dyed with cuttlefish ink. For a true feast, ordering a slow-cooked Peka—meat or octopus roasted for hours under an iron, bell-like dome buried in hot coals—is a masterpiece of Croatian culinary tradition.
