Castles, Canals, and Trenches

Wandering the grand squares of Brussels, stepping into the medieval fairytale of Bruges, tasting authentic Liege waffles, and reflecting on the solemn history of Flanders fields.

Advertisement

Belgium is a country of intense, concentrated contrasts. Within a few hours by train, you can travel from the hyper-modern, bilingual bureaucracy of the EU capital into the ancient, silent trenches of the First World War. Exploring this multi-faceted country required keeping the gear constantly shifting—utilizing the Canon and Sony for those sweeping, gothic cathedral interiors and solemn memorial sites, while relying on the Xiaomi to quickly capture the steam rising off street-side waffle stands and the busy pub life along the canals.

Brussels: The Heart of Europe

The journey anchored in Brussels. Standing in the center of the Grand-Place is a genuinely humbling architectural experience. Surrounded by the opulent, gold-leafed 17th-century guildhalls and the towering spire of the Town Hall, the square completely dominates your field of vision. Yet, for all its political and historical weight, the city has a distinctly relaxed, comic-book-loving soul. It is a place where wandering through narrow alleys inevitably leads you to world-class chocolatiers and bustling bars pouring rich, dark Trappist ales.

Bruges: The Medieval Labyrinth

Taking a train north into West Flanders, Bruges feels like stepping straight onto a medieval movie set. The historic city center is a perfectly preserved network of cobblestone streets, brick gothic churches, and winding waterways. A boat ride through these canals offers the absolute best perspective of the city, gliding past overhanging willow trees and ancient stone bridges. It is a place best explored at a slow pace, losing your sense of direction on purpose and stopping often to sample the local brews.

Liege: The Industrial Soul

Heading east into Wallonia, Liege presents a striking, gritty contrast to the polished fairytale aesthetic of Bruges. This is a city with a robust, industrial edge, anchored brilliantly by the sweeping, futuristic curves of the Liège-Guillemins railway station. But beyond its architecture, the true star of the city is its namesake culinary invention: the Liège waffle. Unlike its lighter Brussels counterpart, this waffle is thick, deeply chewy, and studded with caramelized pearl sugar that crunches perfectly with every single bite.

Flanders Fields: The Memorial Museum Passchendaele 1917

One of the most profound and emotionally heavy stops on this Belgian journey was traveling out to Zonnebeke to explore the Memorial Museum Passchendaele 1917. This immersive historical site forces you to confront the devastating realities of World War I. Walking down into the dim, claustrophobic recreated underground dugouts and traversing the network of trenches brings the abstract history of the textbooks into sharp, terrifying focus. Documenting the artifacts and looking out over the solemn, quiet fields of Flanders served as a powerful, necessary reminder of the incomprehensible sacrifices made upon this very soil.
