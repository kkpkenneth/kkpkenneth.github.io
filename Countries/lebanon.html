Layers of History

An exploration of the resilient pulse of Beirut, the colossal Roman ruins of Baalbek, the historic seaside citadel and ancient Phoenician wall of Byblos, and the proximity of the frontier with Syria—a land defined by its immense history, natural beauty, and a highly complex, often intense security environment that requires constant vigilance.

The Resilient Capital: Beirut

Beirut is a city that defies simple classification. Known for its incredible resilience, it is a fascinating blend of ultra-modern glass skyscrapers, bullet-riddled historic facades, and a vibrant street-level energy that hums day and night. From its legendary dining scene and seaside promenade to the soulful corners of its older neighborhoods, Beirut remains a place where the collision of East and West is felt on every corner.

Roman Colossus: Baalbek

Heading inland into the Bekaa Valley brings you to the mind-bending scale of Baalbek. These Roman ruins are some of the best-preserved and largest in the world. Standing in the shadow of the Temple of Jupiter, with its massive, impossibly large stone blocks, is a humbling experience. It is a place that reminds you of the sheer reach and ambition of the Roman Empire, sitting as a silent, monolithic witness to thousands of years of shifting power.

The Sea Citadel: Byblos

Perched beautifully on the Mediterranean coast, Byblos is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. Its most iconic landmark is the Crusader-era sea citadel—a rugged, stone fortress overlooking the harbor. Just nearby, traces of the ancient Phoenician wall remain, a testament to the city's deep, maritime roots. Walking through the narrow, flower-lined streets of the ancient souq and climbing the stone walls of the citadel to look out over the sparkling sea is a journey through history, from Phoenician origins to Crusader defenses.

Navigating High-Stakes Environments

Traveling in Lebanon involves navigating a highly complex security environment. In regions like the Bekaa Valley or when approaching sensitive frontier areas near the border with Syria, the presence of various non-state actors and local authority figures creates a climate where situations can become tense unexpectedly. My time here included moments of extreme caution and a severe instance of detention by local paramilitary forces occurring just 15 minutes from the International Airport of Beirut; during this time, all of my electronics were confiscated for three days while I was interrogated within an abandoned house. While ultimately clarified as a misunderstanding, these events serve as a stark reminder of the intense, unpredictable nature of travel in areas of significant geopolitical tension, and emphasize the necessity of complete situational awareness for any traveler.

The Lebanese Table: Mezze

Lebanese cuisine is arguably one of the most sophisticated in the region, and it centers on the social tradition of Mezze. It is not just a meal, but an experience: a sprawling array of small plates including smooth hummus, smoky baba ghanoush, crispy tabbouleh, stuffed vine leaves, and kibbeh. Paired with warm, freshly baked bread and a glass of arak, it is a masterclass in fresh ingredients, intricate spices, and the absolute necessity of communal dining.
