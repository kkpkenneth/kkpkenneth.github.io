Nomads of the Steppe

A sweeping journey across Central Asia's giant, from the mountainous, leafy avenues of Almaty and the southern Silk Road warmth of Shymkent, to the Caspian shores of Aktau and the glittering, sci-fi skyline of Astana.

Advertisement

The Cultural Heart: Almaty

Nestled beautifully at the base of the snow-capped Trans-Ili Alatau mountains, the former capital of Almaty remains the undisputed cultural and financial heart of Kazakhstan. Exploring this city means wandering through wide, leafy parks, admiring imposing Soviet-era architecture, and diving into the vibrant, sensory overload of the Green Bazaar. Taking the cable car up to Kok Tobe at sunset provides a stunning panoramic view of the mountains colliding with the sprawling cosmopolitan grid.

The Southern Gateway: Shymkent

Heading south towards the Uzbek border, the climate warms noticeably and the pace shifts in Shymkent. As one of the oldest and most populous cities in the country, it acts as a vibrant crossroads steeped in Silk Road history. The atmosphere here is notably traditional and welcoming, offering authentic, bustling markets and serving as the perfect gateway to explore the ancient mausoleums and ruins scattered across the southern steppes.

The Caspian Frontier: Aktau

Far out to the west, isolated by vast stretches of barren desert, lies the coastal city of Aktau. Built directly on the edge of the Caspian Sea, this unique settlement offers a stark, industrial contrast to the rest of the country. Walking along the rocky seafront promenade provides a surreal view of the world's largest inland body of water meeting the harsh, dramatic landscapes of the Mangystau region.

The Futuristic Capital: Astana

Rising like a glittering mirage from the freezing northern steppes is the modern capital of Astana. The city is a playground of ultra-modern, wildly ambitious architecture. From the golden, glowing sphere of the Bayterek Tower to the massive, translucent tent of the Khan Shatyr, Astana feels like a master-planned monument to the future—a surreal and fascinating testament to the nation's immense wealth and vision.

The Nomadic Feast: Beshbarmak

You cannot travel the vast steppes without partaking in Beshbarmak, the ultimate national dish whose name translates to "five fingers" (as it is traditionally eaten by hand). It is a deeply comforting, heavy meal consisting of boiled meat—often horse or lamb—served over wide, flat, handmade noodles, and ladled with a rich onion broth known as shorpa. It is the absolute centerpiece of Kazakh hospitality and the perfect fuel for traversing such a massive country.
