The Complete Archipelago: A Culinary Journey Through All 47 Prefectures

From the snow festivals of the deep north to the tropical shores of the far south, this archive documents a total immersion into every corner, city, and culinary specialty of Japan.

Advertisement

Very few travelers experience the true scale of Japan. While the neon lights of the golden route captivate the imagination, the soul of the country is scattered across all 47 of its prefectures. This archive is the culmination of traversing the entire archipelago. It is a documentation of distinct regional identities, unforgettable local wildlife interactions, profound historical preservation, and an unparalleled gastronomic diversity mapped through personal culinary discoveries.

Hokkaido & Tohoku: The Wild North

The journey into Japan's vast northern frontier begins in Hokkaido, an expansive landscape where winter shapes the culture. Exploring the capital city of Sapporo requires the hearty fuel found in the region's legendary food scene; nothing beats fighting off the cold with bowls of complex, heavily spiced Soup Curry and intensely savory Sakura Ebi Ramen loaded with tiny, brilliant pink shrimp. Dropping south into the rugged Tohoku region reveals deep, historic traditions. You can feel the energy of the massive Nebuta floats in Aomori while eating custom-built Nokke Don seafood bowls and Crisp Apples. The coastal resilience of Sendai in Miyagi shines through its famous Gyutan (Grilled Beef Tongue) and sweet Zunda Mochi. Neighboring prefectures offer deep comforts: massive Yonezawa Beef meals and fresh Cherries in Yamagata, endless bowls of Wanko Soba and rich Natto Kimchi Ramen in Morioka (Iwate), refined Inaniwa Udon and traditional Hatahata-Zushi in the snowy mountains of Akita, and the unique flavors of Koi no Namero and Fukushima Peach in the samurai stronghold of Aizu-Wakamatsu (Fukushima).

Kanto: The Metropolitan Heart

Moving into the Kanto plain, the energy shifts dramatically toward massive urban sprawl, anchored by the neon-drenched metropolis of Tokyo. Exploring the capital involves marveling at the towering heights of the Skytree, hunting for exclusive merchandise at the Pokémon Center, and indulging in a culinary scene that is unmatched. The Tokyo menu spans master-crafted Sushi, hearty sumo-sized Chanko Nabe, delicate Strawberry Pancakes, fun Pokémon-Themed Dishes, and savory, griddle-cooked Monjayaki. Beyond the capital, the surrounding prefectures provide distinct flavors and retreats. Yokosuka and the Kanagawa coast serve up heavy Sanma Men, naval-inspired Navy Curry, unique Shirasu Ice Cream, and crispy Tako Senbei. To the north, the Kairakuen gardens of Mito (Ibaraki) sit in the homeland of sticky Natto and sweet Chestnut Cake. The ornate shrines of Nikko contrast with the endlessly crispy Gyoza and slurp-worthy Sano Ramen found in Utsunomiya (Tochigi). In Gunma, the therapeutic hot springs are enjoyed alongside wide, flat Himokawa Udon, while Kawagoe in Saitama offers Edo-era streets lined with intricate Bonsai Dessert and Kawahaba Udon. Finally, the coastal plains of Chiba are defined by the indulgent, all-you-can-eat Disneyland Buffet.

Chubu: The Alpine Spine

The center of Honshu is defined by the towering peaks of the Japan Alps and a rugged coastal beauty. On the Sea of Japan side, heavy snowfall in Niigata nourishes the unique seaweed-infused Hegi Soba. Toyama offers delicate, translucent Shiroebi (White Shrimp) and deeply salty Black Ramen, while the preserved geisha districts of Kanazawa in Ishikawa are matched by luxurious Kanimeshi and decadent Gold Leaf Ice Cream. Further down the coast, Fukui yields fun novelty Dinosaur Popsicles alongside simple, satisfying Sauce Katsudon and grated-radish Echizen Soba. Moving inland, the shadow of Mt. Fuji falls over Kofu in Yamanashi, where bubbling iron pots of Miso-Simmered Houtou Noodles warm travelers. Nagano’s alpine castle in Matsumoto is surrounded by fields producing unique delicacies: Horse Sashimi, massive fried Sanzoku-Yaki, stuffed Oyaki, Hoba Yaki, and even Wasabi-Flavored Ice Cream. The traditional thatched roofs of Takayama in Gifu offer incredibly marbled Hida Beef and Grilled Ayu (Sweetfish). On the Pacific coast, Shizuoka commands the production of perfectly glazed Charcoal-Grilled Unagi, and the industrial powerhouse of Nagoya in Aichi boasts a bold flavor profile with rich Miso Udon and peppery Tebasaki chicken wings.

Kansai: The Cultural Core

Kansai is the historic and spiritual heartline of Japan, balancing ancient temples with chaotic, food-obsessed entertainment hubs. The history runs deep in Kyoto, where relaxation is found soaking in the historic tiles of Funaoka Onsen, followed by delicate Herring Soba, elegant Matcha Ice Cream, and fresh Mochi. Nara provides another incredible step back in time, where wild deer bow to visitors beneath ancient pagodas, accompanied by heritage foods like persimmon leaf-wrapped Kakinoha Sushi and comforting Chagayu (tea gruel). In stark contrast, Osaka thrives on massive, high-energy entertainment. The street food culture here is legendary, centering on deep-fried Kushikatsu, rich Oyster Ramen, and my absolute favorite, molten Takoyaki. Rounding out the region, Kobe and Himeji in Hyogo offer flawless Kobe Beef, pressed squid Ikayaki, and soft, dashi-dipped Akashiyaki. Mie protects the sacred shrines of Ise alongside soft Ise Udon and tender Matsusaka Beef, while Shiga guards the premium cuts of Omi Beef around Otsu. Finally, the spiritual mountain paths of Wakayama are fueled by Wakayama Panda Ramen, massive portions of Jiro-Style Ramen, sour Umeboshi Ice Cream, and traditional Meharizushi.

Chugoku & Shikoku: Western Reaches and the Pilgrim's Island

The western stretch of Honshu (Chugoku) and the neighboring island of Shikoku offer a deeply traditional and maritime-focused journey. In Chugoku, the vast sand dunes of Tottori provide a backdrop for luxurious Michelin-Recommended Tottori Beef and savory Gyukotsu Ramen. The water city of Matsue in Shimane serves up tiered boxes of Warigo Soba and restorative Shijimi Clam Soup. Okayama shines with vibrant Sawara no Koko Sushi, Flying Fish Ramen, and jewel-like White Peaches. Hiroshima stands as a powerful city of peace, beloved for its layered Hiroshima-Style Okonomiyaki and massive, plump Grilled Oysters, while at the tip of the island, Shimonoseki in Yamaguchi prepares translucent Fugu (Pufferfish), aromatic Beer Curry, and sizzling Kawara Soba cooked on hot roof tiles.

Across the Seto Inland Sea, the island of Shikoku is fiercely proud of its independent culture and distinct culinary exports. The energetic Awa Odori dances of Tokushima are brightened by zesty Sudachi Lime. Kagawa may be the smallest prefecture, but Takamatsu is globally recognized for perfectly chewy Sanuki Udon, spicy roasted Honetsuki-Dori (Bone-In Chicken), and green Olive Somen. In Ehime, bathing in the ancient Dogo Onsen of Matsuyama is paired with fresh Tai Meshi (Sea Bream Rice) and sweet Mikan Oranges. Finally, the rugged, seafaring city of Kochi celebrates its coastal roots with thick, garlic-garnished slices of Katsuo no Tataki (Seared Bonito)—which firmly stands out as my absolute favorite dish in all of Japan.

Kyushu & Okinawa: The Subtropical South

The deep south of Japan vibrates with volcanic energy and distinct subtropical identities. On the island of Kyushu, the energetic riverbank stalls of Fukuoka serve up iconic, milky-pork-broth Tonkotsu Ramen, spicy Mentaiko, and the regional hybrid, Saba Mentaiko. The pottery towns of Karatsu in Saga offer beautifully marbled Saga Beef and creamy Go Dofu. The historic foreign influences in the harbor of Nagasaki resulted in hearty, seafood-rich Champon Noodles, crispy Sara Udon, and sweet, Portuguese-inspired Castella Cake. Watched over by its massive black castle, Kumamoto is famous for garlic-heavy Kumamoto Ramen, spicy lotus root Karashi Renkon, and ultra-fresh Ika-no-Ikizukuri. Geothermal power defines Oita, where the steam vents of Beppu and Yufuin cook vegetables into Jigokumushi to eat alongside rich Bungo-Gyu Mabushi. Miyazaki brings a sun-drenched coastal vibe with incredibly juicy Chicken Nanban and the fun, flowing noodles of Nagashi-Somen. Kagoshima, sitting across from an active volcano, prides itself on premium Kurobuta Pork, massive bowls of condensed-milk Shirokuma Shaved Ice, deep-fried Satsuma-Age, rare Black Satsuma Chicken Sashimi, and volcanic-themed Sakurajima-Yaki. Finally, jumping completely off the main islands lands you in Okinawa. The tropical capital of Naha possesses a completely unique culinary DNA, featuring oceanic Umi-Budo (sea grapes) and the localized fusion favorite, Taco Rice.

This archive represents the total completion of the Japanese archipelago, spanning all 47 prefectures.
