The Long Road to the Balkans

An overland and oversea journey crossing the Ionian Sea into Patras, exploring the ancient heart of Athens, marveling at the north in Thessaloniki and Meteora, and finally passing through Serres into Bulgaria.

Advertisement

The Sea Crossing: Italy to Patras

The gateway to this Greek expedition was by water. Taking the slow, scenic ferry from the eastern coast of Italy across the deep blue of the Ionian Sea brings you directly into the bustling port city of Patras. Arriving by sea sets the perfect, relaxed pace for entering the Peloponnese, allowing you to watch the rugged Greek coastline slowly emerge on the horizon before diving into the chaos of the mainland.

The Ancient Heart: Athens

Heading east, Athens is a city where history literally spills out onto the modern streets. The sheer weight of walking beneath the shadow of the Acropolis is humbling. But the real charm of Athens lies in its vibrant, gritty modern culture—getting lost in the narrow, graffiti-lined alleys of Plaka or Monastiraki, grabbing a quick gyro, and feeling the energy of a city that has survived for millennia.

The Northern Giants: Meteora & Thessaloniki

Pushing north, the journey took a breathtaking detour. *(Note: If you were captivated by the cave dwellings of Matera in Italy before your ferry ride, Greece’s geological equivalent is the sky-high floating monasteries of Meteora!)* Standing atop these massive sandstone pillars feels completely otherworldly. Further up the coast, Thessaloniki offers a cooler, more laid-back alternative to Athens. As Greece's cultural capital, it sits beautifully on the Thermaic Gulf, blending Byzantine history with a phenomenal waterfront promenade.

The Borderlands: Serres to Bulgaria

The final leg of the Greek journey veered into the quiet, authentic northeast region of Macedonia. The town of Serres served as the perfect, off-the-beaten-path launchpad. Surrounded by rolling mountains and devoid of massive tourist crowds, it provided a peaceful final night in Greece before crossing the nearby border straight into the heart of Bulgaria.

The Ultimate Comfort: Moussaka

Greece has an incredible culinary repertoire, but nothing holds a candle to a masterfully baked Moussaka. It is the absolute king of Greek comfort food. A rich, bubbling casserole of velvety eggplant, perfectly spiced ground meat (usually beef or lamb), and potatoes, all crowned with a thick, golden, and deeply satisfying layer of perfectly browned béchamel sauce. Finding a traditional taverna and eating a slice of this while it’s still scorching hot is a mandatory travel experience.
