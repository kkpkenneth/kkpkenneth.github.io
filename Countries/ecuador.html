The Andes to the Enchanted Isles

Journeying from the dizzying altitudes of Quito down to the humid coastal bustle of Guayaquil, before ultimately setting sail for the greatest wildlife sanctuary on Earth: the Galapagos Islands.

Advertisement

Ecuador is a country of staggering geographic diversity packed into a remarkably compact footprint. The journey demanded constant adaptation—both in terms of wardrobe and camera gear. The Xiaomi captured the colonial plazas of the mainland cities with ease, but the heavy Canon with the long telephoto lens was the undisputed star of this trip, required for documenting what I can confidently say is my absolute favorite place in the world.

Mainland Contrasts: Quito and Guayaquil

The expedition began high in the Andes in Quito. Sitting at nearly 3,000 meters above sea level, the air is thin, crisp, and surrounded by dramatic volcanic peaks. Wandering through its spectacularly preserved historic center feels like stepping back in time. From there, the route descended sharply to the tropical, sea-level heat of Guayaquil. Ecuador’s largest city and chief port pulses with commercial energy, offering sweeping views along the Malecón 2000 and serving as the humid, bustling gateway to the Pacific.

My Favorite Place in the World: The Galapagos Islands

Nothing can truly prepare you for the Galapagos. It is an evolutionary wonderland where wildlife exhibits absolutely no fear of humanity. Arriving at the wind-swept airstrip of Baltra, the expedition immediately launched into an island-hopping voyage that completely redefined my understanding of nature.

Every island offers a completely distinct ecological fingerprint. We explored the lush highlands of Santa Cruz alongside giant tortoises, and watched sea lions lounging on the park benches of San Cristobal. Navigating to the younger, volcanic western islands of Isabela and Fernandina revealed landscapes that look freshly forged from the earth's core, covered in vast colonies of marine iguanas. Further out, Genovesa provided a dizzying spectacle of red-footed boobies and frigatebirds, while Espanola delivered the breathtaking sight of waved albatrosses taking flight. Finally, anchoring near Floreana brought the journey full circle, blending incredible flamingo lagoons with the island's fascinating, bizarre human history.

Encebollado and Fresh Coastal Ceviche

The transition from the Andes to the ocean completely shifts the culinary landscape. While Quito thrives on hearty mountain fare like llapingachos (potato patties) and slow-roasted pork, the coast and the islands belong entirely to the sea. The ultimate hangover cure and national treasure of Guayaquil is Encebollado—a rich, deeply flavorful albacore tuna and yuca soup topped with pickled red onions. Out in the Galapagos, lunches consisted of incredibly fresh, bright Ecuadorian Ceviche, typically featuring shrimp or fish swimming in a tangy, citrusy tomato broth, always served alongside a bowl of toasted corn nuts (tostado) or plantain chips.
