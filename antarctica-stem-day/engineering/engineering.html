Building an Igloo Model

An Engineering & Clay Modeling Exercise inspired by Polar Architecture

Background: The Engineering of Igloos

While Antarctica is primarily known for scientific research stations rather than indigenous populations, traditional dome-shaped shelters called igloos were masterclasses in Arctic engineering built by the Inuit. Despite being made purely of compressed snow blocks, they are remarkably structural.

Igloos utilize a catenary or dome arch design, which naturally distributes weight outward and downward rather than straight down. This allows the structure to withstand heavy winds and accumulating snow loads. In this engineering exercise, we substitute snow blocks with clay blocks to study how these self-supporting structural physics work on a miniature scale.

Materials Needed for the Exercise

Modeling Clay / Air-Dry Clay

White or grey clay to mimic snow bricks.

Plastic Ruler or Butter Knife

For cutting uniform, rectangular clay blocks.

Cardboard Base

A small square piece (approx. 20x20 cm) to build your model on.

Step-by-Step Construction Guide

Step 1: Brick Production

Roll out your clay into even flat strips (about 1 cm thick). Use your ruler or plastic knife to cut out dozens of uniform rectangular "ice" blocks. Consistency in size is crucial for proper stacking.

Step 2: Laying the Foundation

As demonstrated in traditional building methods, mark out a circle on your cardboard base using a string setup [00:01:17]. Lay your first row of clay blocks flat along this circle, leaving a small gap where the entrance tunnel will eventually go.

Step 3: Spiraling and Inward Sloping

Authentic igloo blocks are shaved into a spiral wedge pattern so they lean inward [00:01:45]. For your clay model, gently angle each subsequent row inward, overlapping the joints like bricks to maintain structural integrity.

Step 4: Sealing the Dome

As the dome narrows at the top, carve a final keystone block to plug the very center hole. Press the clay gently at the seams to bond the blocks together, simulating how wind and minor melting fuse real snow blocks.

Step 5: The Entrance Tunnel

Construct a small, short rectangular tunnel leading into the main dome [00:01:23]. In real environments, this low tunnel prevents heavy polar winds from blowing directly inside and traps cold air below the living floor level.
