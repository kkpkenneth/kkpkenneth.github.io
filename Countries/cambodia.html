The Empire and the Mekong

Confronting the heavy history of the capital in Phnom Penh and getting lost within the ancient, sprawling stone ruins of Siem Reap.

Advertisement

Cambodia is a country of profound resilience and staggering archaeological scale. Navigating its extremes required a versatile camera setup. The Sony was essential for pulling details out of the dimly lit, vine-choked temple corridors, the Canon with a wide lens captured the sheer magnitude of the ancient empires, and the Xiaomi was perfect for documenting the chaotic, vibrant street life and bustling night markets without being intrusive.

The Weight of History: Phnom Penh

The journey began in the capital, Phnom Penh, situated at the busy confluence of the Mekong and Tonlé Sap rivers. It is a city that vibrates with chaotic motorbike traffic and vibrant riverfront energy, yet it carries an incredibly heavy, somber past. Visiting the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum (S-21) and the Choeung Ek Killing Fields is a profoundly moving, difficult, but absolutely essential experience to understand the resilience and strength of the modern Cambodian people. Contrasting this dark history is the glittering golden roof of the Royal Palace and the labyrinthine, bustling aisles of the Central Market.

The Ancient World: Siem Reap

Heading northwest brings you to Siem Reap, the undisputed gateway to the ancient Khmer Empire. Waking up at 4:30 AM to capture the sunrise behind the iconic lotus towers of Angkor Wat is a photographic rite of passage that lives up to every bit of the hype. But the magic extends far beyond the main complex. Getting lost among the massive, enigmatic smiling stone faces of the Bayon temple, or watching the massive silk-cotton tree roots physically swallow the ruins of Ta Prohm, makes you feel like an active explorer uncovering a lost world hidden deep within the jungle.

Fish Amok, Lok Lak, and Kuy Teav

Cambodian (Khmer) cuisine is subtle, fragrant, and heavily relies on fresh herbs and river fish. The absolute pinnacle of the local diet is Fish Amok—a delicate, creamy curry made with coconut milk and lemongrass, traditionally steamed to a mousse-like consistency and served inside a folded banana leaf. For a hearty, meat-heavy option, Lok Lak provides perfectly stir-fried, peppery beef served over fresh tomatoes and lettuce, often topped with a fried egg. And to start the day like a local, nothing beats pulling up a plastic stool at a street market for a steaming bowl of Kuy Teav, a rich pork broth noodle soup that perfectly fuels you up for a long day of temple trekking.
