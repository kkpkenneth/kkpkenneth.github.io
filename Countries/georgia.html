The Heart of the Caucasus: Ancient Balconies and Legendary Feasts

Documenting a country where rugged overland routes from the south open up into dramatic canyons, deep Orthodox history, and one of the greatest culinary traditions in the world.

Advertisement

Crossing overland from Iran sets the stage for an incredible shift in culture and geography. Arriving in Georgia feels like stepping into a deeply complex crossroads of Eastern Europe and Western Asia. It is a nation deeply rooted in ancient Orthodox traditions, dramatic mountain landscapes, and a legendary, unapologetic approach to hospitality and food that makes it a heavyweight chapter in this global archive.

Tbilisi: The Capital in the Canyon

The energy of the country radiates outward from its vibrant capital, Tbilisi. The city is beautifully chaotic, built along the steep banks of the Kura River. Walking through the Old City reveals narrow, cobblestone streets lined with decaying, intricately carved wooden balconies that overhang the pathways below. Dropping down into the Legvtakhevi Canyon right in the middle of the city exposes natural waterfalls and the famous domed brick roofs of the traditional sulfur bathhouses.

By night, the city transforms. The local bar and hostel scenes here are electric, blending international backpackers with the deeply ingrained local culture. Whether passing landmarks like the towering Parliament of Georgia or sketching ideas on a public transit train winding through the city, Tbilisi constantly feeds a traveler's creative energy.

The Supra: Unrivaled Culinary Power

You cannot document Georgia without dedicating serious focus to its food. The cuisine here is heavy, communal, and completely unforgettable. A standard feast (or supra) is a test of endurance. It usually begins with the iconic Khachapuri—specifically the boat-shaped Adjarian style—filled with molten cheese and topped with a raw egg yolk and a heavy slice of butter that you stir together with torn crust.

Alongside the bread comes dense, spiced Kubdari meat pastries and massive platters of Khinkali. These large, meticulously pleated dumplings are filled with spiced meat and hot broth, requiring a very specific technique to eat by hand without spilling a drop. To wash down the rich spreads of dips, roasted meats, and stuffed cabbage, the locals pour endless glasses of dark, dry Saperavi Wine, produced using ancient clay vessel techniques that date back thousands of years.

The Katskhi Pillar & Beyond

Venture outside the capital, and the rugged landscape of the Caucasus reveals staggering historical oddities. Among the most breathtaking sights is the Katskhi Pillar—a sheer, natural limestone monolith that rises 130 feet straight up into the air. Incredibly, a tiny, ancient Orthodox church is perched right on its peak, serving as an isolated hermitage. Witnessing these gravity-defying structures in the wild perfectly encapsulates the resilient, ancient spirit of Georgia.
