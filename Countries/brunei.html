The Abode of Peace

Exploring the gilded mosques of Bandar Seri Begawan, navigating the historic water homes of Kampong Ayer, and crossing the great bay bridge into the pristine rainforests of Ulu Temburong.

Advertisement

Brunei is a quiet, incredibly wealthy sultanate that feels entirely insulated from the chaotic backpacker trails of its Southeast Asian neighbors. The pace of life here is unhurried and deeply respectful. Documenting the sheer scale of the country's oil-funded architecture required relying on the wide-angle capabilities of the Sony and Canon, while the Xiaomi proved perfect for capturing the fast, jarring boat rides across the river and the intricate details of the stilt houses.

The Capital and the Water Village: Bandar Seri Begawan & Kampong Ayer

The journey anchors in the capital, Bandar Seri Begawan. The city is spotless, dominated by the magnificent, gold-domed Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque rising elegantly out of an artificial lagoon. But the true cultural heartbeat of the city lies just across the river.

Hailing a wooden water taxi brings you into the sprawling labyrinth of Kampong Ayer. Often referred to as the "Venice of the East," this is the world’s largest floating village. Entire communities—complete with schools, clinics, and mosques—are built on wooden stilts over the Brunei River. Walking the rickety boardwalks offers a fascinating glimpse into a traditional way of life that has thrived on the water for centuries, standing in stark contrast to the modern wealth of the mainland.

Across the Bridge: Ulu Temburong National Park

To experience Brunei's untouched wilderness, you must head to the Temburong exclave. This journey is now made incredibly seamless by the monumental Sultan Haji Omar Ali Saifuddien Bridge—a staggering 30-kilometer feat of engineering that curves right across the Brunei Bay. Once across, you swap the car for a traditional longboat to head deep into Ulu Temburong National Park.

Because the sultanate's wealth comes from offshore oil rather than logging, this rainforest remains one of the most pristine, fiercely protected jungles in Borneo. Climbing the dizzying aluminum scaffolding to the top of the canopy walkway puts you face-to-face with a sprawling, unbroken ocean of green. The air is thick with the sounds of hornbills and gibbons, providing a sensory overload that perfectly counterbalances the quiet order of the capital.

Ambuyat and Nasi Katok

You cannot leave Brunei without trying its national dish, Ambuyat. Made from the starchy interior of the sago palm trunk, it is a thick, glue-like substance that you twirl around a specialized bamboo fork (called a chandas). It has almost no flavor on its own; the magic happens when you dip it into a fiery, sour cacah (sauce) made from fermented shrimp and local chilies. For a fast, deeply satisfying everyday meal, Nasi Katok is the undisputed champion. Sold on almost every street corner, this simple parcel of steamed rice, a piece of fried chicken, and a generous smear of spicy sambal wrapped in brown paper is the ultimate Bruneian comfort food.
