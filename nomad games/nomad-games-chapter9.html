Chapter 9

The Clock Tower

A thick, cool mist hung over the River Thames as Kenjiro navigated the cobblestone paths of London. Towering above the city's historic skyline stood the magnificent Elizabeth Tower, holding the legendary bell known as Big Ben. Its golden clock face pierced through the grey morning fog, casting a warm, steady glow over Westminster Bridge.

"Now this is a change of pace from the neon lights of Luckland," Kenjiro remarked, pulling his coat tighter against the brisk wind. He glanced sideways at the plush size AI turtle perching on his shoulder.

"Indeed," Kame-kun whirred quietly, his optical sensors adjusting to the low-light conditions. "My atmospheric sensors detect high humidity, and historical data indicates we are standing near the political and temporal center of the United Kingdom. It is highly probable that powerful ET users congregate in areas with such dense historical resonance."

Before hunting for any potential threats or allies, Kenjiro's stomach let out a loud, undeniable growl. Taking Kame-kun's advice to blend in, they stopped at a bustling corner pub near the edge of the river.

Kenjiro eagerly devoured a steaming, newspaper-wrapped portion of traditional fish and chips. The battered cod was perfectly crisp, heavily dashed with sharp malt vinegar, and served alongside thick-cut chips that radiated warmth into his chilled hands.

"Incredible. The vinegar cuts right through the richness," Kenjiro mumbled happily with a full mouth, wiping grease from his chin.

"A robust choice for a damp morning, young sir, though it is usually preferred with a side of mushy peas," a crisp, exceedingly polite voice echoed from a few paces away.

Kenjiro turned to see a tall, immaculate gentleman leaning lightly on a silver-tipped walking umbrella. He wore a perfectly tailored tweed three-piece suit, a crisp pocket square, and a silver pocket watch chain draped across his waistcoat. In his gloved hand, he effortlessly balanced a bone-china teacup filled with steaming Earl Grey.

"Uh, thanks. I'm Kenjiro," he said, swallowing quickly and standing up.

"Arthur Pendelton, at your service," the gentleman said, giving a slight, elegant bow. His sharp blue eyes flicked immediately to the plush size AI turtle perching on his shoulder. "I see you travel with a rather unique companion. And a heavy aura. You are a Telepath, are you not?"

Kenjiro tensed, immediately dropping his half-eaten chip. If this man could sense his Extraordinary Trait without even making physical contact, he was incredibly dangerous.

"Peace, my boy," Arthur chuckled softly, taking a refined sip of his tea. "I have no quarrel with you. I am simply a man who watches the clock. You see, the flow of time in this city is my domain. My ET allows me to perceive the temporal ripples of those destined to alter the future."

"Temporal perception is theoretically impossible without violating the laws of thermodynamics," Kame-kun interjected skeptically.

Arthur smiled, reaching into his pocket and pulling out his silver watch. He didn't press a button, but merely ran his thumb over the glass. In the blink of an eye, the rain that had just begun to fall froze in mid-air. The bustling crowds on Westminster Bridge paused like statues. The only things moving were Arthur, Kenjiro, and Kame-kun.

"You stopped time..." Kenjiro whispered in awe, poking a suspended raindrop.

"Only for three seconds. A gentleman never overstays his welcome in the fabric of the universe," Arthur noted lightly as the world suddenly slammed back into motion, the rain splashing down onto the cobblestones. "I approached you, Kenjiro, because your path is about to intersect with deeply dangerous individuals. There are others seeking the secrets you hold. Seek the archives beneath the British Museum. You will find the answers you need there, before the hour strikes."

Before Kenjiro could ask for clarification, Big Ben began to toll, its massive, booming chime echoing across the river. Dong. Dong. Kenjiro instinctively looked up at the great clock face. When he looked back down, Arthur Pendelton was entirely gone, leaving behind only the faint, lingering scent of bergamot tea in the London fog.
