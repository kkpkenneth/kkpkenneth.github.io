The Long White Cloud: 38 Days Through Aotearoa

A photographic journey across the North and South Islands, from Auckland's urban harbors to the rugged Southern Alps.

Advertisement

New Zealand, or Aotearoa, is a land defined by dramatic contrasts—geothermal wonders, alpine peaks, and a deep-rooted Māori heritage. Across visits in 2016, 2019, and 2023, the most extensive chapter occurred in mid-2023, covering 38 days from June 13 to July 20. This expedition was a deep dive into the natural and cultural soul of the country, moving from the metropolitan energy of Auckland down through the geothermal heart of the North Island, across the Cook Strait, and into the glacial landscapes of the South.

The North Island: Geothermal Wonders and Māori Heritage

The journey began in Auckland, the "City of Sails," where the skyline is dominated by urban sophistication and coastal views. Moving south, Rotorua offered a direct encounter with the geothermal pulse of the earth at the Te Puia Māori Arts & Crafts Institute, where Māori carvings and steaming geysers create a landscape unlike any other. The culinary highlights here included fresh fish and chips, a staple of the Kiwi road trip.

In Wellington, the nation's capital, the focus shifted to biodiversity and art. At Zealandia Te Māra a Tāne, I documented the rare Takahe birds, while the Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa provided a masterclass in Māori art and history. The North Island leg also featured the rolling green hills of Waitomo and Hobbiton, landscapes that feel as if they were pulled directly from a cinematic world.

The South Island: Glacial Lakes and Coastal Wilds

Crossing the Cook Strait into Picton, the scenery opened into the vast, turquoise reaches of Queen Charlotte Sound. The South Island is a study in raw nature; in Kaikōura, I documented pods of dolphins playing in the cold Pacific waters. Further south, Christchurch revealed a city in transition, from the restoration of its historic Cathedral to the vibrant life within the Riverside Market.

The Southern Alps dominated the inland route. At Lake Tekapo, the glacial blue waters were backdropped by snow-capped peaks, and a meal cooked on a hot stone provided a warmth that matched the scenery. The coastal journey continued through the misty landscapes of Moeraki and into Oamaru, where I captured a perspective of the rugged coastline from the edge of a cliff.

Northland: The Quiet Life in Maungakaramea

The final chapter of the 2023 expedition took place in Northland, specifically in Maungakaramea. Life here was defined by the domestic and the natural—gardens overflowing with vibrant greenery, dogs resting by the fireplace, and home-cooked lasagna. This leg included visits to Kaiwaka and Whangārei, documenting a side of New Zealand that is slower, greener, and deeply peaceful before departing from Auckland Airport.
