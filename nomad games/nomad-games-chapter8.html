Chapter 8

Macau

After a roaring, high-speed turbojet ride that lasted for hours, slicing through coastal mist and sea foam, the duo finally arrived in Macau. The entire cityscape was a dazzling labyrinth of neon-lit mega-resorts and towering golden monoliths, their glass facades reflecting a million lights and echoing endlessly with the mesmerizing, clinking sounds of dropping coins and spinning wheels.

"This place is unreal!" Kenjiro beamed, his eyes wide as he took in the opulent architecture and bustling plazas illuminated by the warm, glowing neon. He reached up absentmindedly to pat the plush-sized AI turtle perching on his shoulder.

"I advise caution, Kenjiro," Kame-kun whirred, his optical sensors scanning the extravagant entertainment complexes. "This environment is entirely structured around the mathematical concept of probability. It is a calculated labyrinth built to test, and often exploit, cognitive bias regarding chance."

Before Kenjiro could process the warning, a deafening chime erupted from a glittering gaming terminal nearby. Ding ding ding ding ding! A massive cascade of digital credits flooded the screen, raining tangible gold tokens into the tray below. Standing casually in front of the machine was a young woman wearing a sleek, modern streetwear jacket. Resting right beside her winning terminal on the glossy console was an agile, sharp-eyed navigation AI—a sleek, midnight-black plush cat with glowing neon-amber optical sensors.

"Payout velocity and variance metrics verified within optimal thresholds," the plush cat pilot purred smoothly, its digital cadence sharp and measured. "Though your vocal jubilation marginally exceeds recommended discretion levels, partner."

"Finally. Took long enough," she smirked, scooping her winnings into a heavy pouch. She turned, noticing Kenjiro staring in awe. "Hey there. I'm Lucky Star, and this is Nyx."

"Did you just calculate the exact algorithm to win that?" Kenjiro asked, thoroughly impressed.

"Calculate? Nah, math gives me a headache," Lucky Star laughed. "My ET is sheer luck. I just touch the machine, and I win."

She leaned in close, checking over her shoulder before dropping her voice to a conspiratorial whisper.

"But you have to go low-key. If the pit bosses realize my ET guarantees a win, they'd boot me out in a heartbeat. I have to act like I've lost a fortune—dumping chips into duds for hours and acting completely frustrated—every single time before I finally let myself hit a jackpot with my ability."

"Objection," Kame-kun chimed abruptly from Kenjiro's shoulder, his sensors flashing a critical blue. "Luck is merely a perceptual illusion born from statistical variance. What you call sheer luck is simply a retroactive cognitive bias applied to favorable outcomes. It cannot be classified as a true ability."

"Empirical models indicate a high probability of a logical impasse," Nyx the cat pilot added, twitching its plush ears with subtle amusement. "Yet anomalous outcomes frequently bypass standard regression algorithms."

"Big words for a little turtle," Lucky Star fired back, crossing her arms with a grin. "But you're missing the point. You look at a roulette wheel and see a fixed mathematical probability. I look at it and see a wave I can ride. It's not bias; it's a tangible force. The universe isn't just a giant spreadsheet, it's a living casino."

"Your analogy is fundamentally flawed," Kame-kun countered smoothly. "A casino operates on strictly deterministic mechanics disguised as chance. If a system possesses enough known variables—such as exact velocity, air resistance, and surface friction—every random event is entirely predictable. You do not possess luck; you likely possess an undetected micro-kinetic ability that unconsciously manipulates these physical variables to ensure a win."

"So you're saying I'm Laplace's demon? I'm subconsciously calculating the trajectory of every atom in the room?" Lucky Star rolled her eyes, scoffing. "Please. You're trying to force the infinite into a neat little box. What about chaos theory? The butterfly effect? You can't measure the unmeasurable, so your logic just pretends it doesn't exist."

"The inability to measure a complex phenomenon does not grant it mystical properties," Kame-kun insisted, his tone perfectly flat. "It simply highlights the current limits of our computational models. To rely on luck is to surrender your agency to the unknown."

"And to rely entirely on logic is to become a prisoner of the known!" Lucky Star stepped forward, pointing a finger at the AI. "You can't calculate a miracle, Kame-kun. My ability doesn't follow causality—it relies on teleology. I don't care how the gears in the machine turn to hit the jackpot. I only command the final destination. I skip the cause and go straight to the effect. That is the true essence of luck."

Kenjiro stood between them, his mind buzzing. He watched the fierce, highly animated debate unfold between the strictly logical AI and the wildly fortunate gambler under the soft, refracted glow of the resort lights. Neither could fully disprove the other's worldview, yet the discussion was profoundly inspiring, forcing Kenjiro to consider how the world might perfectly balance both rigid, calculated order and wild, inexplicable chaos.

"Well, this has been stimulating, but I've got to go convincingly lose at the baccarat tables for a while," Lucky Star sighed happily, stretching her arms. She reached into her bag and suddenly tossed a heavy pouch of gold tokens into Kenjiro's arms.

"Whoa! What is this for?" Kenjiro stammered, his knees buckling slightly under the sudden weight.

"Consider it a sponsorship for your journey! Just an act of sheer generosity," she winked, walking backward into the blinding crowd alongside Nyx. "May the odds be ever in your favor!"
