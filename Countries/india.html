A Subcontinent of Chaos and Color

An immense, multi-regional expedition spanning the political weight of New Delhi, the tiger reserves of the central states, the deep spiritual history of the east, the sprawling megacities of the west, and the vibrant tech-hubs of the south.

Advertisement

The Northern Arc: Delhi, Agra & Beyond

The journey commenced in the overwhelming, magnificent sprawl of New Delhi, a city where centuries-old tombs sit in the shadows of modern overpasses. The classic route pushed southeast to Agra for the mandatory, yet eternally breathtaking, sunrise view of the Taj Mahal, and west to the sweeping, pink-hued palaces of Jaipur. But the north is vast: venturing up to the modernist, grid-planned streets of Chandigarh provided a sharp architectural contrast, while the cool, mirror-like lakes of Srinagar in Kashmir felt like an entirely different world. Further grounding the region in deep mythology were the sacred, chaotic, and joyous temple towns of Mathura and Vrindavan.

The Heart of the Jungle: Tala, Mocha & Katni

Diving deep into the central state of Madhya Pradesh, the concrete gave way to dense sal forests. Passing through the essential railway junction of Katni, the expedition set up basecamps in Tala (the gateway to Bandhavgarh) and Mocha (bordering Kanha National Park). These early morning safari drives through the mist, tracking pugmarks in the dust in the hopes of locking eyes with a wild Bengal tiger, are among the most thrilling experiences the subcontinent has to offer.

The Spiritual East: Kolkata to Gaya

The eastern leg was steeped in intellect and profound history. It started in the colonial-era grandeur and intellectual hum of Kolkata. Tracing the Ganges westward into Bihar, the route hit Patna before exploring the absolute marvel of Nalanda, the ruins of one of the world's most ancient universities. The eastern journey culminated in the profound, hushed reverence of Gaya and Bodh Gaya, where pilgrims from across the globe gather beneath the Bodhi tree.

Megacities and Coasts: West to South

Crossing to the west coast, the sheer economic engine of India was on full display in the textile hub of Ahmedabad and the sensory overload of Mumbai. Slipping south along the coast provided a much-needed, sun-soaked breather on the beaches of Goa. The final push headed into the deep south, navigating the sweltering heat and Dravidian temple architecture of Chennai, and marveling at the ancient, monolithic rock carvings of Mahabalipuram. Finally, heading inland to the cooler, tree-lined avenues of Bengaluru offered the perfect bookend to the trip—especially catching up and spending some quality downtime giving a massive shout out to the Bengaluru gang.

The Culinary Landscape: The Great Indian Thali

With a country this vast, picking a single dish is impossible. The only true way to eat your way across the subcontinent is by ordering a Thali. This grand platter is a beautiful, localized snapshot of wherever you happen to be sitting. Whether it's loaded with rich paneer gravies and buttery naan in the north, or fiery sambars, coconut chutneys, and massive mounds of rice on a banana leaf in the south, the Thali is the ultimate, endless feast of Indian diversity.
