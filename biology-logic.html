Logical Reasoning in Biology

Test your deductive reasoning and understanding of biological systems.

1. The Lethal Allele (Genetics)

In a certain species of mouse, the allele for yellow fur (Y) is dominant over the allele for black fur (y). However, the homozygous dominant genotype (YY) is embryonic lethal (the embryos die before birth). If two yellow mice mate, what will be the phenotypic ratio of their surviving offspring?

View Answer & Logic
Answer: 2 Yellow : 1 Black.

Logic: A normal Mendelian cross of heterozygotes (Yy x Yy) yields a 1:2:1 ratio (1 YY : 2 Yy : 1 yy). Because the YY offspring die before birth, they are removed from the surviving population pool. You are left with 2 Yy (Yellow) and 1 yy (Black).

2. The Shrinking Cell (Cellular Biology)

An animal cell is placed in a liquid solution. Over the next hour, the cell visibly shrivels. What can you logically deduce about the solute concentration of the solution compared to the inside of the cell, assuming the membrane is impermeable to the solute?

View Answer & Logic
Answer: The solution is hypertonic (has a higher solute concentration than the cell).

Logic: Through osmosis, water moves from an area of lower solute concentration to an area of higher solute concentration to achieve equilibrium. Because the cell shriveled, water must have exited the cell. Therefore, the surrounding solution must have had a higher concentration of solutes than the cell's interior.

3. The Persistent Disease (Evolution & Population Genetics)

A specific genetic disease is fatal in early childhood for individuals who inherit two copies of the recessive allele. Despite being heavily selected against, the allele remains relatively common in certain populations. Logically, what is the most likely evolutionary reason for this?

View Answer & Logic
Answer: Heterozygote advantage.

Logic: If a trait persists despite being fatal, the carriers of the trait (heterozygotes who have one normal allele and one disease allele) must have a survival advantage in their specific environment over those who have no disease alleles at all. A classic example is the sickle cell trait providing resistance to malaria.

4. The Toxic Lake (Ecology)

A non-biodegradable chemical is dumped into a lake. It is absorbed by algae, which are eaten by small fish, which are in turn eaten by large birds of prey. In which organism would you expect to find the highest concentration of the chemical per gram of tissue?

View Answer & Logic
Answer: The large birds of prey.

Logic: This demonstrates biomagnification. Because the chemical is non-biodegradable, it is not broken down or excreted. As you move up the trophic levels, predators must consume a large biomass of prey to survive, accumulating all the toxins stored in that prey into their own, smaller total biomass.
