Chapter 1

THE PILOT

Kenjiro didn’t hate his job. In a city like Hong Kong, where the skyline is a jagged heart monitor of ambition and stress, just not hating your 9-to-5 is considered a luxury. But tonight, after a grueling 12-hour shift, all he wanted was the quiet sanctuary of his apartment.

He sat heavily on the edge of his bed on the 16th floor of the concrete jungle, looking out the window at the glowing, relentless sprawl of Kowloon. He was exhausted, rubbing his temples while the dull hum of the city vibrated through the glass. He was comfortable in his routine, even if that routine was slowly grinding him into dust.

Then, his phone buzzed on the nightstand.

It wasn't a standard notification. The screen overrode his lock screen, displaying a stark black background with a single line of plain white text:

Do you want to play a game? [ YES ] [ NO ]

Kenjiro blinked. Spam? A viral marketing campaign? Out of sheer, exhausted curiosity, his thumb tapped YES.

The screen refreshed instantly.

Do you want to travel around the world? [ YES ] [ NO ]

He hesitated. He looked around his cramped 16th-floor apartment and thought about how big the world is compared to his minimal home. The air inside felt heavy with the mundane, inescapable reality of his life. Again, curiosity won. He tapped YES.

His phone didn't refresh. Instead, it emitted a high-frequency, piercing sound that bypassed his ears and vibrated directly against his skull. A violent electric shock arced from the glass screen into his thumb. The neon lights outside his window smeared into a blinding white streak, and Kenjiro’s world went dark.

The Ivory Tower

Kenjiro woke up on a cold, perfectly smooth floor.

He was no longer in the concrete jungle. He was in a pristine, blindingly white circular room with no windows and a single closed elevator door. The walls looked like polished bone or flawless ivory.

"Yoo-hoo! Sleeping Beauty! Wakey wakey!"

Kenjiro scrambled backward. A small, floating stuffed turtle—no larger than a melon, made of soft green plush fabric with a stitched, patchwork shell and oversized, incredibly expressive embroidered eyes—was paddling through the air toward him. Despite looking exactly like a child's toy, it completely defied gravity.

"Welcome to the Nomad Games, Traveler 008! I am your pilot, Kame-kun!" The little plush turtle did a cheerful, mid-air backflip. Then, it floated a little closer, its embroidered eyes somehow shifting into a judgmental squint. "Oh wow, you look even more exhausted in person. The dark circles under your eyes are tragic. Don't worry, I'll try to use small words so your brain doesn't overheat."

"Where am I?" Kenjiro stammered, staring at the floating stuffed animal. "What is this?"

"Your goal is to travel around the world and complete all the missions of the games!" Kame-kun chirped brightly, entirely ignoring his panic. "Right now, you're on the 16th floor of the Ivory Tower. Your first mission is to pass this test and descend to the ground floor."

A holographic keypad materialized next to the elevator door.

"The test has officially started!" Kame-kun cheered, waving his little plush legs. "Try not to die of a brain aneurysm on the math questions, okay? Good luck!"

The Descent: 16 Floors of Logic

Floor 16: The Primer
Prompt: What comes next in the sequence: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32...?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "64."

The door chimed and opened. Kame-kun floated past him. "Aww, look at you! You know your multiplication tables. Good boy!"

Floor 15: Pattern Recognition
Prompt: O, T, T, F, F, S, S, E, N, _ ?

Kenjiro counted under his breath. One, Two, Three, Four... "T. For Ten."

Chime.

Floor 14: Cognitive Reflection
Prompt: A bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost?

His exhausted brain screamed 10 cents. But he caught himself. "5 cents," he typed.

Chime. Kame-kun swam circles around his head. "Ooh, you almost said 10 cents, didn't you? I could literally smell the smoke coming out of your ears, 008!"

Floor 13: Spatial Reasoning
Prompt: A pond has a patch of lily pads. The patch doubles in size every day. If it takes 48 days to cover the entire pond, how long does it take to cover half?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "47 days."

Chime.

Floor 12: Linguistic Lateral Thinking
Prompt: What is seen in the middle of March and April that can't be seen at the beginning or end of either month?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "The letter R."

Chime.

Floor 11: The Trap of Assumption
Prompt: A man and his son are in a terrible car accident. The father dies. The son is rushed to the hospital. The surgeon looks at the boy and says, "I can't operate on him, he is my son." Who is the surgeon?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "The boy's mother."

Chime.

Floor 10: Deductive Logic
Prompt: If 1=3, 2=3, 3=5, 4=4, and 5=4, what does 6 equal?

Kenjiro realized it was letter counts. One (3), Two (3), Three (5). "3."

Chime. "Not bad," Kame-kun yawned, projecting a tiny holographic hammock and lying his soft, stuffed body inside it. "Still painfully slow, but not bad."

Floor 9: Contextual Physics
Prompt: You are in a freezing, dark cabin. You have one match. There is a candle, a wood stove, and a kerosene lamp. What do you light first?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "The match."

Chime.

Floor 8: Abstract Wordplay
Prompt: A butcher at the market is 5 feet 10 inches tall and wears size 11 shoes. What does he weigh?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "Meat."

Chime.

Floor 7: The Trick of Words
Prompt: A man pushes his car to a hotel and immediately tells the owner he is bankrupt. Why?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "He's playing Monopoly."

Chime.

Floor 6: Sequential Paradox
Prompt: What has keys but can't open locks, space but no room, and you can enter but not go outside?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "A keyboard."

Chime.

Floor 5: Probability Optimization
Prompt: You have 50 red marbles, 50 blue marbles, and two jars. How do you divide the marbles to maximize the probability of drawing a red marble blindly?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "Put one red marble in one jar, and all the rest of the 99 marbles in the other."

Chime.

Floor 4: Mathematical Wordplay
Prompt: I have two coins totaling 30 cents. One of them is not a nickel. What are the two coins?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "A quarter and a nickel. One is not a nickel, but the other is."

Chime.

Floor 3: The Liar's Paradox
Prompt: Two guards stand before two doors. One door leads to the exit, one leads to death. One guard always lies, one always tells the truth. You can ask one question to one guard. What do you ask?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "'Which door would the other guard point to?' And then I take the opposite door."

Chime.

Floor 2: The Grid
Prompt: There are three switches outside a closed room, and three lightbulbs inside. You can only enter the room once. How do you know which switch operates which bulb?

Kenjiro’s Answer: "Turn on switch 1 for ten minutes. Turn it off. Turn on switch 2. Enter the room. The bulb that is on is switch 2. The bulb that is off but warm is switch 1. The cold bulb is switch 3."

Chime. Kame-kun clapped his little stuffed feet together. "Wow, you actually got that! I was already drafting your obituary."

Floor 1: The Final Check

Kenjiro stepped out of the elevator onto the ground floor. His head was pounding. The final terminal stood before an imposing set of heavy double doors. Kame-kun floated up, resting his soft chin on top of the terminal screen, looking unusually quiet.

There was no math equation. No wordplay. Just a single blinking cursor and a question:

You were given a choice. What is the only reason you are here?

Kenjiro froze. He almost typed "Because I pressed Yes." He almost typed "My phone shocked me." His finger hovered over the holographic keyboard. If he got this wrong, what would happen?

He thought back to his cramped apartment in the concrete jungle. He didn't hate his job, but he didn't love his life either. What was the exact emotion that forced his thumb to press that button?

He typed a single word: Curiosity.

The screen flashed green. The heavy double doors hissed and slid open, revealing a blindingly bright sunlight that washed over the sterile white floor.

Mission Accomplished

As his boots hit solid, natural ground, a translucent digital interface abruptly expanded in Kenjiro's field of vision.

[ MISSION 1 COMPLETED: THE IVORY TOWER ]
FUNDS DEPOSITED: $1,000 USD

A sharp, incredibly satisfying audio cue played in his head. Ka-ching.

"Congratulations, Traveler 008!" Kame-kun cheered, zipping out of the tower and doing a series of ecstatic cartwheels in the air, his plush fabric spinning wildly. "The money has been deposited into your traveler's account. This is your budget for the next leg of your journey."

Kenjiro looked around. The air smelled of salt and lush tropical vegetation. The relentless skyline of Kowloon was completely gone.

"I honestly didn't think you'd make it," Kame-kun said, fluttering down to hover right at Kenjiro's eye level. The little stuffed turtle smiled sweetly. "You really beat the odds... and my incredibly low expectations of your species! Prepare yourself, Kenjiro!"

The little plush turtle pointed a soft, stubby arm toward the horizon.

"Mission 2: Emerald Island!"
