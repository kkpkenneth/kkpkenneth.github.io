Bosnia and Herzegovina

Where Empires Meet

Tracing the visible scars of recent history in Sarajevo and witnessing the incredible reconstruction of the iconic bridge in Mostar.

Advertisement

Bosnia and Herzegovina is a profoundly moving country to explore. It sits exactly where the architectural and cultural fault lines of the Ottoman and Austro-Hungarian empires collided, resulting in a fascinating, complex society. Documenting this region required a careful, respectful lens. The Canon and Sony were essential for capturing the grand sweeps of the rebuilt cities and the stunning surrounding valleys, while the Xiaomi was perfect for capturing the intricate, hammered copper details hidden within the narrow bazaar alleyways.

Sarajevo: The Jerusalem of Europe

Sarajevo is a city defined equally by its rich diversity and its tragic recent history. Walking through the Baščaršija (the old bazaar) feels like stepping into Istanbul, with the smell of strong coffee and grilled meat hanging in the air. Yet, just a few streets away, the grand architecture shifts abruptly to Viennese elegance. The scars of the 1990s siege are still visible—bullet holes pattern many building facades, and the "Sarajevo Roses" (mortar craters filled with red resin) serve as somber memorials on the sidewalks. Visiting the Tunnel of Hope, the narrow underground passage that was the city's only lifeline during the siege, is a claustrophobic but essential experience to understand the resilience of the local people.

Mostar: The Rebuilt Bridge

Traveling south through the stunning, rugged mountains brings you to Mostar, a city heavily defined by the Neretva River running fiercely through its center. The focal point of the city is undoubtedly the Stari Most (Old Bridge). Originally built by the Ottomans in the 16th century, it was completely destroyed during the war in 1993, only to be meticulously reconstructed stone by stone in 2004. Standing on the steep, polished stones of the arch and watching members of the local diving club plunge 24 meters into the freezing river below is a breathtaking tradition that symbolizes the enduring, stubborn spirit of the city.

Ćevapi and Bosnian Coffee

The food here is deeply comforting, meat-heavy, and meant to be shared. The undisputed king of the culinary scene is Ćevapi—small, heavily seasoned, grilled minced meat sausages. They are always served tucked inside a warm, soft flatbread called somun, accompanied by a massive pile of chopped raw onions. You will rarely find a bad plate of it anywhere in the country.

Equally important is the ritual of Bosnian Coffee. Do not call it Turkish coffee here, though the preparation is similar. It is served in a traditional copper pot (džezva) on a small tray, accompanied by a glass of water and a sugar cube. The process of pouring, skimming the thick foam, and slowly sipping the incredibly strong, dark liquid is an essential, daily part of the social rhythm.
