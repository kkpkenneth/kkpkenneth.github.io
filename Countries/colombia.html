The Liquid Rainbow and a Gritty Reality

Navigating the intense highs and absolute lows across Bogotá, the salt mines of Zipaquirá, the Caribbean heat of Barranquilla and Cartagena, the calm of Medellín and Guatapé, witnessing the magic of La Macarena, and exiting the country from the jungle outpost of Leticia.

Advertisement

Colombia is a country of staggering natural beauty and profound, undeniable grit. It is a place that demands constant vigilance. Knowing when to pull out the heavy Canon for landscape shots and when to keep the Xiaomi discreetly tucked away was a daily, high-stakes calculation. It was not the safest expedition on the roster, but its raw natural wonders left a permanent mark.

High Altitude Realities: Bogotá and Zipaquirá

The journey started in the sprawling, high-altitude capital of Bogotá. While the city holds incredible museums and street art, it is also where I was robbed—a harsh, immediate reminder that safety here is never guaranteed and your guard must stay up. Just a short trip outside the city limits, however, the atmosphere shifts completely as you descend into the massive, subterranean Salt Cathedral of Zipaquirá. Carved entirely out of an active halite mine deep underground, the silent, softly lit caverns provided a much-needed, awe-inspiring contrast to the chaos of the capital.

Caribbean Hustle: Barranquilla and Cartagena

Heading north to the Caribbean coast brought crushing heat and a completely different rhythm. Barranquilla offered an unapologetic, loud, and bustling industrial energy. Moving down the coast to Cartagena, you are met with postcard-perfect, walled colonial beauty and blooming bougainvillea. Unfortunately, beneath that picturesque exterior lies a very aggressive tourist hustle; experiencing direct extortion here heavily overshadowed the historic charm, proving once again that Colombia’s coastal cities require eyes in the back of your head.

The Fine and the Phenomenal: Medellín, Guatapé, and La Macarena

Heading inland to the Aburrá Valley, Medellín and the nearby colorful lakeside town of Guatapé were perfectly fine. The weather was pleasant, and climbing the massive monolithic rock (El Peñón) in Guatapé provided a smooth, scenic respite from the stress of the coast.

But the undisputed, absolute favorite experience of the entire Colombian expedition lay deep in the central plains: La Macarena. This remote town is the gateway to Caño Cristales, known globally as the "Liquid Rainbow." Trekking out to see this river bloom in vibrant, surreal shades of red, yellow, blue, green, and black—thanks to the unique Macarenia clavigera plant—is one of the most spectacular, otherworldly natural phenomena on the planet.

The Jungle Exit: Leticia

The Colombian chapter concluded at the extreme southern tip of the country in the humid, isolated frontier town of Leticia. Cut off from the rest of Colombia by impenetrable jungle, this outpost sits right on the Tres Fronteras (where Colombia, Brazil, and Peru meet). From here, the expedition officially left Colombian soil by boarding an Amazonian ferry to push deeper into the continent along the world's greatest river.

Bandeja Paisa, Arepas, and Ajiaco

Colombian food is heavy, comforting, and designed to keep you moving. The absolute heavyweight champion is the Bandeja Paisa—an intimidating, massive platter consisting of beans, rice, chicharrón (pork belly), minced meat, chorizo, a fried egg, plantains, and avocado. It is a meal you have to mentally prepare for. For daily sustenance, warm, cheese-stuffed Arepas are sold on nearly every street corner. And when the cold rain hits the high altitudes of Bogotá, nothing fixes morale faster than a steaming bowl of Ajiaco, a thick, rich chicken and potato stew served with capers and a heavy pour of cream.
